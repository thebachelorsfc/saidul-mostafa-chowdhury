Experience · Teaching

The Teacher

Teaching is my profession: the first of my worlds, and the one around which the others are built. Since April 2026, I have served as Assistant Teacher (English) at Madrasha Khairul Ummah in Chittagong, teaching English to students from Grades 3 to 10. It is patient, daily work: helping students find their words, and then the confidence to stand behind them.

My standards originate in the classroom. I prepare thoroughly, I state only what I mean, and I do not waste the time of others. The same hand is visible later, in the club I founded.

My responsibilities, as recorded in my professional profile, are to design lesson plans aligned with curriculum standards, to monitor student progress and provide constructive feedback, and to support classroom management and student engagement.

Experience · Leadership & Community

The Founder

In 2026, in Khulshi, I founded The Bachelors FC: an apolitical, non-profit football club for bachelor youth in need of a place to belong. The club has no sponsors and no stadium; it has a growing brotherhood, a motto, and a standard — character above trophies.

I lead the club as its Founder and Captain, with responsibility for its standards, its programme and its people, both on and off the pitch.

A club isn't measured by the trophies it wins — but by the men it saves from themselves.

Experience · Sport & Leadership

The Captain

On 29 October 2026, I assumed the captaincy of the club, taking the armband and the goalkeeper's position in the #99 shirt. I guard the goal as I guard the club itself: in an organised manner, calmly under pressure, as the final line upon which every other player may rely.

The club's first football match, played on 6 November 2026 at Diesel Colony Play Ground, was against Madrasha Khairul Ummah FC, the team of the school at which I teach. Colleagues and students in the classroom by day; opponents on matchday. Both teams performed well, the occasion was enjoyed by all, and the brotherhood between the two sides grew stronger.
